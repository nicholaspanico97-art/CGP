Capability profile

bars are the difficulty frontier · numbers are the published score

The number on each row is the published score on whatever suite is currently in use for that domain — not a readout of the bar beside it. A benchmark is a distribution over task difficulty, so it saturates on its own schedule and gets replaced by a harder one; a superscript marks how many times that has happened. Two labs at the same frontier score differently, because elicitation differs, and a lab that optimises for the suite scores higher than it can actually perform.

A model is not one number. Each bar is one domain, bought with a share of the training mixture and the data to feed it — a domain with no data is compute set on fire. Domains marked est have hand-set capability curves with no public score history behind them.

Benchmarks

AA composite, then each suite · leader in each is bold

AA is the composite: each suite's score inverted back to the task difficulty it implies, weighted and rescaled so 50 is a GPT-4-class model. It keeps climbing where an average of raw scores would saturate — and it shows amber and goes blind when every suite underneath it has been exhausted and the harder ones have not arrived. The small percentage is coverage: how much of the index a lab is measurable on at all. A media specialist scores well and covers little.

These are the numbers the market prices — and the only numbers a rival can see. A benchmark is a distribution over task difficulty, so each suite saturates on its own schedule and is replaced by a harder one; the arrow is the change since three months ago. Strategies

drawn at the start of the game, one per lab, with variation inside each

Revenue share is the wrong scoreboard for most of these. An open-weights lab is not trying to out-earn an enterprise vendor — it is trying to keep the field close and stay the developers' default. So each lab is scored against its own objectives, shown here at the end of the run.

In a game you would not see this panel. Each lab draws a strategy and a set of parameters inside it, and you infer who is doing what from what they ship, what they charge, and what they are visibly not doing. A lab that goes quiet for two years is telling you something.

The race, by domain

every lab's capability over the decade · one chart per domain

Dots mark a shipped pretrained model; stars mark a run that landed well above trend. Flat stretches are real: a run that comes in below what a lab already sells gets shelved rather than released, so capability never steps down — it waits.

The badge on each chart counts how many times the lead actually changed hands, and how much of the decade the dominant lab held. A domain where one lab holds 90%+ for a hundred months is a runaway, not a race — this panel exists to make that visible rather than arguable.

Markets

share of each segment, at the current month

Each segment is gated on a domain. Below the gate a lab has no product at all — not a worse one. Gates are on capability, never on the date: a segment opens the month someone clears it.
